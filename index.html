Ch. 06 — Questions

The questions worth asking.

Every shift starts with skepticism. These are the questions agencies and clients always ask — and what we've learned to answer.

How is AI production different from traditional production?

Traditional production: Casting → Location → Crew → Shoot → Post → 3–6 weeks, with budgets of VND 55–200 million for a 30s TVC (Vietnam market).

AI production: Brief → Character setup → per-shot generation → Post. With the right scope and assets ready, the timeline is typically 7–10 days and can significantly cut logistics costs versus a traditional shoot. Hybrid/live action is still used when the brief needs real talent, a product demo or physical proof.

When to use AI: concept testing (animatic), social content, key visuals, storyboards. When you still need to shoot: broadcast 4K TVC, talent-driven campaign, complex product demo.

📖 Lemonlight: AI Video vs Traditional — An Honest Comparison

Why does an AI commercial need per-shot production instead of generating a whole sequence?

Because agencies and clients don’t approve “is the AI impressive” — they approve casting, product, claim, packshot, legal, lighting and continuity. Each element can change independently after a round of feedback.

Per-shot production isolates exactly what needs fixing: shot 3 relights, shot 5 swaps talent, the end-frame adjusts the packshot — approved shots stay intact. That keeps scope, timeline and revision cost easier to control, while protecting what the brand needs the audience to remember: product, claim, packshot and CTA.

One-take / multishot generation is still very useful for concept exploration, social experiments or a locked sequence. But when a project carries many approval rounds and brand accountability, the per-shot workflow is the safer architecture.

→ The difference isn’t who is “better at AI,” but whether the workflow is optimized for creative speed or for control and commercial accountability.

What are the current technical limits of AI video?

• Resolution: 4K is available (native on newer video models such as Kling 3.0 Native 4K, or via AI upscale) — 1080p is still the cost/quality sweet spot for digital/social; 4K for hero/broadcast carries a surcharge

• Clip length: 5–10 seconds per clip (some models up to ~15–20s) — the full film is assembled from multiple clips in post

• Hard-to-control details: accurate on-screen text, hand/finger detail, small fast-moving objects

• Randomness: the same prompt can yield different results — iteration is needed to reach the target quality

• Content policy: Some AI platforms restrict content involving children or violence — the team will advise an alternative approach

📖 Cliptics: Video Quality — AI Generated vs Filmed Content

Can a character’s face stay consistent throughout?

Yes — but the quality depends on the input data:

• With a talent reference shoot (40–80 photos per character) → very high face consistency, little iteration

• With only reference photos from the agency → still achievable but needs more iteration

This is why we recommend a talent reference shoot — it raises quality and provides a talent release for safe publishing.

Image rights and AI law in Vietnam?

Image rights: an AI character may carry facial features from a reference. To publish safely:

✅ Photoshoot real talent + sign a talent release

✅ Or use a 100% fictional character

If the agency supplies the reference photos → the agency is responsible for confirming usage rights.

AI labeling: under Vietnam’s Artificial Intelligence Law (134/2025/QH15, effective 01/03/2026), AI-generated content must be labeled on release. AI Studio advises on compliant labeling.

📖 Vietnam AI Law 134/2025 — Vietnam Legal Library

What if the creative direction changes midway?

Unlike traditional production (where you can reshoot a single angle), changing the concept in AI means all footage has to be regenerated from scratch. Cost and time are equivalent to starting over.

→ We recommend locking the creative direction before generation begins. Fine-tuning feedback (color, pace, timing) along the way is expected — it doesn’t require regenerating footage.

What is an AI animatic for? Does it replace the traditional animatic?

It can replace a traditional illustrated animatic when the goal is photoreal concept validation — but it does not replace every form of previs. An AI animatic lets teams test story, performance, camera and production design before committing budget to a shoot; because it looks closer to the intended film, client feedback becomes more specific and decisions become faster.

Once the concept is approved → it can move to a real shoot (with clear direction) or be developed further in AI into a social film.

📸 Why shoot talent references for AI production?

A talent reference shoot (40–80 photos per character) delivers:

✅ Significantly higher face consistency across shots

✅ A talent release → safe, legally compliant publishing

✅ Fewer revisions → saves time for both sides

✅ Director involvement → more production-grade output

Reference-shoot cost: quoted by number of talent. It's an add-on service — not mandatory but strongly recommended for projects intended for release.

How long is the timeline from brief to delivery?

• AI Animatic / Social Film: typically 7–10 working days with a locked scope

• Key Visual: 3–5 days

• Storyboard: 3–5 days depending on frame count

The timeline depends on how fast feedback comes from the agency/client. Late feedback → the delivery date shifts accordingly. Rush (under 5 days) → negotiated separately.
